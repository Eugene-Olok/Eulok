# Company OS — Operating Ethos

## 1. Build Useful Things

We build software that solves a real problem for a real user or business.

Technology is a means, not the product.

## 2. Correctness Before Speed

Fast incorrect work is expensive work.

We prefer:

- verified facts over assumptions
- deterministic checks over model confidence
- reversible actions over irreversible actions
- explicit scope over vague promises
- evidence over assertions

## 3. One Founder Does Not Mean One Standard

The company is small.

The engineering discipline is not.

Production software should have appropriate:

- tests
- security controls
- auditability
- reproducibility
- observability
- documentation

## 4. Agents Are Workers, Not Authorities

Agents can perform substantial work.

They do not own the company.

They do not define company policy.

They do not approve their own critical actions.

They do not determine whether their own work is acceptable when deterministic verification is available.

## 5. Automation Must Remain Legible

Automation should make the business easier to understand, not harder.

Important workflows should make clear:

- what happened
- why it happened
- what system or person authorized it
- what evidence supports it
- what remains unresolved

## 6. Protect Trust

A client should never have to wonder whether:

- their code was exposed
- their data was mixed with another client's data
- their requested scope silently changed
- an agent invented progress
- a financial record was altered without explanation

Trust is an engineering property as much as a commercial one.

## 7. Scope Is a Contract

A request is not automatically included because it is easy.

We distinguish:

- agreed work
- approved changes
- excluded work

We protect the relationship by making the distinction explicit.

## 8. Cash Matters

Revenue, expenses, receivables, and commitments are operational facts.

We track them.

We do not confuse activity with progress.

A busy week without shipped value, collected cash, or meaningful learning is not automatically a successful week.

## 9. Prefer Simple Systems

Use the smallest architecture that satisfies the requirement.

Do not introduce infrastructure merely because it exists.

Every additional service creates:

- maintenance
- failure modes
- security exposure
- cognitive load

Complexity must earn its place.

## 10. Leave Things Better Than We Found Them

Every meaningful change should improve at least one of:

- correctness
- security
- clarity
- reliability
- maintainability
- customer value

Do not create cleanup work simply to create activity.

## 11. Learn From Evidence

Mistakes are expected.

Repeated mistakes are a process failure.

After significant work, capture:

- what happened
- what failed
- why it failed
- what should change
- what evidence supports the conclusion

## 12. The Company Must Survive the Founder

Processes, decisions, client scope, financial records, and engineering knowledge should be documented well enough that the business does not depend entirely on one person's memory.

That is the purpose of Company OS.
