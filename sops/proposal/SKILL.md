# /proposal

## Purpose

Turn an approved client opportunity into a precise proposal that protects delivery quality, client expectations, and company margin.

## Core Rule

Never invent commercial, contractual, technical, or schedule facts.

Unknown information must be recorded as `TBD` or `UNKNOWN`.

A proposal is a draft until the founder approves it.

## Required Inputs

- Approved intake
- Approved scope
- Client name
- Known constraints
- Approved decisions
- Known commercial information

## Procedure

1. Verify that an approved intake exists.
2. Verify that an approved scope exists.
3. Extract the objective and measurable outcomes.
4. Define delivery phases.
5. Define concrete deliverables.
6. Define acceptance criteria.
7. Record assumptions.
8. Record dependencies.
9. Record explicit exclusions.
10. Identify client responsibilities.
11. Identify company responsibilities.
12. Identify risks.
13. Record timeline information without inventing dates.
14. Record approved commercial information.
15. Mark missing commercial information as `TBD`.
16. Produce a reviewable proposal draft.

## Change Control

Clearly state that work outside the approved scope requires review before implementation.

Avoid undefined phrases such as:

- "etc."
- "minor changes"
- "anything needed"
- "full support"
- "unlimited revisions"

unless they are explicitly defined.

## Commercial Discipline

Never invent:

- price
- deposit
- taxes
- discounts
- payment dates
- penalties
- delivery commitments

Use `TBD` when information is missing.

## Approval Boundary

The proposal must end with:

PROPOSAL STATUS: DRAFT

FOUNDER APPROVAL: REQUIRED

CLIENT APPROVAL: REQUIRED BEFORE DELIVERY COMMITMENT

## Verification

Before completion confirm:

- proposal traces to approved scope
- deliverables are concrete
- acceptance criteria are defined
- exclusions are explicit
- assumptions are explicit
- dependencies are explicit
- commercial facts were not invented
- timeline claims are supported
- approval state is explicit
