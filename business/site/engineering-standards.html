Working agreement

Clear commitments. Verifiable delivery.

A client should know what is being built, how success will be assessed, what remains uncertain and what happens at handover.

Before work begins

  • We agree the problem, intended outcome, deliverables and exclusions in writing.
  • Acceptance criteria, dependencies, material risks, timeline and commercial terms are documented in the proposal.
  • Unknowns are surfaced rather than replaced with assumptions. Where discovery is needed, we define it as a separate bounded engagement.
  • Implementation begins after the scope and commercial terms are approved by both sides.

During delivery

  • Material changes are discussed and approved before they change the agreed scope.
  • Testing and review are appropriate to the system, the impact of failure and the agreed acceptance criteria.
  • Errors, exceptions, unresolved risks and known limitations are made visible; successful-looking output is not treated as proof by itself.
  • Consequential financial actions and ambiguous decisions retain appropriate human review.

Security and client information

Access is limited to what the engagement requires. Credentials are handled separately from source code, and client information is not intentionally mixed across engagements. Client code and confidential data are not sent to free or unapproved AI services; restricted information is not processed through AI workflows. Any AI use that could affect client information should be discussed and bounded by the engagement's agreed requirements.

Specific processing, retention, access, and deletion arrangements depend on the systems involved and are agreed before sensitive data is transferred.

Before handover

  • Deliverables are checked against the agreed acceptance criteria.
  • We identify what was tested, what was not tested, known limitations and outstanding issues.
  • We provide the agreed source, documentation, operating instructions and access-transfer steps.
  • Maintenance, response times and ongoing support are covered by explicit terms rather than implied availability.

How to assess the work

We distinguish a target from a measured result, and a test run from a guarantee. Public examples and technical notes will state their context and limitations; client outcomes are published only with permission and supporting evidence.
