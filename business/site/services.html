What a client receives

A deliverable is more than a commit.

Written scope

Inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, dependencies and change control.

Acceptance criteria

Observable conditions the client can use to verify the agreed result.

Verification evidence

Checks appropriate to risk, plus honest disclosure of what remains unverified.

Ownership and handover

Deployment notes, access responsibilities, documentation and next steps.

Security boundaries

Data access, secrets, roles and external approvals identified before implementation.

Support boundary

Maintenance scope, response windows and exclusions agreed separately from a build.

Local context

Designed around real operating constraints.

Uganda-based work can include mobile-money flows, EFRIS-related integration requirements, low-bandwidth users and teams whose source of truth is currently a spreadsheet. Each integration depends on current provider capabilities, client permissions, onboarding and technical requirements.

We do not claim URA accreditation, payment-provider certification, compliance certification or a track record that has not been verified.

Fit before commitment

Start with the constraint, not a technology list.

Before proposing a solution, we establish what is failing today, who uses the process, what systems are involved, what must not break, who approves the work and what evidence will define success.
