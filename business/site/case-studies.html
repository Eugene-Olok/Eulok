We will add case studies after completed engagements have been accepted and the client has approved publication. We will not invent logos, testimonials, outcomes or metrics to fill the space.

What a published case study will show

  • The original problem and the constraints that mattered.
  • The agreed scope, including what was deliberately excluded.
  • What changed and why that approach was chosen.
  • How the delivered work was verified, with the limits of the evidence.
  • Measured outcomes only where a credible baseline and method exist.
  • Known limitations, operational considerations and lessons learned.

A reconciliation workflow should expose exceptions.

This small illustrative dataset shows the kinds of outcomes a reconciliation process should distinguish. These are fabricated demonstration records—not client data, a live integration or a claim about production performance.

What this illustrates

  • Matching rules should be explicit and reproducible.
  • Unmatched records, duplicates and amount conflicts are visible outcomes—not silently treated as success.
  • Ambiguous financial decisions require review; corrections should preserve an audit trail.
